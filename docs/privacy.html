Privacy Policy for SajdaTime

Last updated: 3 October 2026 · Disclaimer

SajdaTime has no accounts, shows no advertising, and keeps your location on your phone. If you choose to, it can send usage counts to Google Analytics so the developer can see how the app is used. That is off unless you switch it on, and it never includes your location, your school of thought or your prayer settings.

What the app needs, and why

SajdaTime works out prayer times and the direction of the Qibla from your approximate location. To do that it asks for the approximate location permission (ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION). It reads your location only while the app is open, never in the background.

Your coordinates are saved on your own device so the app still works with no signal. They are never sent to us or to anyone else. SajdaTime runs no server of its own, and your coordinates are never part of the optional usage counts described below.

You can refuse the location permission entirely and type a city name instead. The app is fully usable that way.

When something leaves your device

Two things can. One happens only if you type a city name and your device cannot resolve it. The other happens only if you switch on the optional usage counts. Both are described in full below.

Typing a city name

If you choose to type a city name rather than use your location, that name has to be turned into coordinates. The app first tries to do this using the lookup service built into Android itself. If your device cannot resolve it, and only then, the city name you typed is sent once to Open-Meteo, a free geocoding service, which sends back the coordinates.

What is sent is the place name you typed and nothing else — no identifier, no device information, no account, nothing that says the request came from you. The app tells you this is going to happen before it does it. Nothing about that lookup is stored by SajdaTime beyond the coordinates it gives back, which stay on your device.

To be completely straight with you about the part we do not control: like almost every web service, Open-Meteo keeps ordinary server logs, and their terms say those logs are deleted after 90 days. Those logs will contain the request and the internet address it came from. We have no access to them and we receive nothing back from Open-Meteo except the coordinates. We mention it because a policy that quietly stops at our own front door would not be an honest one.

Open-Meteo's data is used under the CC BY 4.0 licence.

Optional usage counts

SajdaTime can send usage counts to Google Analytics for Firebase, a service provided by Google, so the developer can see how many people use the app, how often, for how long, and where people get stuck during setup, and so make it better. It is off by default. You are asked once while setting up (or once after updating, if you installed the app before this option existed), and you can change your mind at any time in Settings, under About, with the switch called “Share usage counts”. If you say no, nothing about the app changes.

What is sent if you say yes

  • How many people use the app, how often and for how long (sessions and time the app is open)
  • Which of the three main screens you open: Times, Qibla or Settings
  • Which setup steps you reach, and whether you allow notifications and location, as a yes or no each. For exact alarms it records only whether they are allowed when setup ends
  • Your phone model, Android version, app version and language
  • Your approximate area, usually the country and sometimes the city, which Google works out from your internet connection. According to Google, IP addresses are not logged or stored in Analytics

All of it is tied to a random ID created on your phone. It is not your name, and there is no account or email behind it.

What is never sent

Your GPS position or coordinates, the place you type, your school of thought or madhab, your calculation method, your alert and prayer settings, and anything you have typed. This is a fixed rule of the app, and nothing else can be added to the list above without this page changing first. Using a prayer app can say something about a person’s faith, which is why this is a clear choice that you make, and why we never record which school of thought you follow.

Who receives it, and where

Google, acting as our analytics provider. Google says Firebase may process and store data anywhere Google or its agents maintain facilities, which can be outside the UK. We have switched off the Google settings that would let Google use the data for its own purposes, advertising or personalisation. The app removes the advertising permissions and does not use the advertising ID. We do not sell the data or use it for advertising. You can read Google’s own description.

How long it is kept

Detailed records are kept for up to 14 months. Summary reports that combine many users may be kept for longer. They cannot be traced to you.

Stopping, and what that does not undo

Switch “Share usage counts” off and the app stops sending anything and clears its random ID from your phone. Data already sent stays with Google until it expires. Because the ID is random and the app never holds your name, the developer cannot tell which records are yours, so we cannot offer to delete your records on request. If you reinstall the app, your choice is not carried across: you are asked again and a new random ID is created.

Our basis for this

We rely on your consent, given by choosing Yes, and you can withdraw it at any time as described above. The consent text you are shown while setting up is the same text you see if you turn it on later in Settings.

If you have a Wear OS watch

When a paired watch is present, your settings and location are sent to it over Google's local Data Layer so the watch can work out prayer times on its own. This travels directly between two devices that belong to you. It does not go to a server, and it does not reach us.

What SajdaTime never does

  • No user accounts, sign-in, or email collection
  • No usage measurement unless you switch on the optional usage counts above, and no crash reporting
  • No advertising and no advertising identifiers
  • No advertising or marketing code. The only analytics code in the app is Google’s library for the optional usage counts, and it is told not to collect until you say yes
  • No selling of data and no use of it for advertising
  • No backup and no device transfer: Android's automatic backup is switched off, and the app is also excluded from device-to-device transfer, so your saved location is not copied to a backup server and is not carried across when you set up a new phone

Children

SajdaTime is suitable for all ages and is not directed at children specifically. It has no accounts and asks for no personal details. The optional usage counts are off by default for everyone, are explained in plain language before anyone can turn them on, and contain nothing that identifies a child or anyone else. If you are under 18, ask a parent or guardian before you switch them on.

Permissions in full

  • ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION — to calculate prayer times and the Qibla. Foreground only.
  • POST_NOTIFICATIONS — to tell you when a prayer time arrives.
  • SCHEDULE_EXACT_ALARM — so a prayer alert arrives at the right minute rather than whenever the system feels like it.
  • RECEIVE_BOOT_COMPLETED — to restore your prayer alerts after the phone restarts.
  • INTERNET — for the optional city-name lookup and, only if you switch them on, the optional usage counts, both described above.
  • com.google.android.finsky.permission.BIND_GET_INSTALL_REFERRER_SERVICE — added by Google’s analytics library. It lets Google Play tell that library which link brought you to the app, for the optional usage counts.
  • ACCESS_NETWORK_STATE, WAKE_LOCK and FOREGROUND_SERVICE — added by the libraries the app uses (Android’s background work library, which keeps your prayer alerts scheduled, and Google’s analytics library). SajdaTime does not use them for anything else.
  • The app removes the advertising permissions that Google’s library would otherwise add (AD_ID and the Android ad-services permissions).
  • On the watch, WAKE_LOCK — so the tile can refresh its countdown.

Disclaimer

SajdaTime is a helper, not a religious authority.

These times are not supplied by a mosque, a scholar, or any authority. Your own phone works them out from the position of the sun, using calculation methods that Islamic authorities and scholars have published. The app is written by one person who is not a Mufti, Aalim, or an expert in fiqh, and it was built with the help of artificial intelligence. Software can carry faults, a phone can hold the wrong location or the wrong clock, a phone saving its battery can delay an alert, and the author can simply be wrong.

So please take what the app shows as a calculation and not a ruling. Where SajdaTime and your mosque disagree, follow your mosque. If a time or a direction looks off to you, or you are unsure, ask them or someone else qualified to advise you.

Mosques do not all agree on when a prayer starts, and Isha varies the most. In Britain and further north the gap between one published method and another can exceed an hour, because scholars measure the fading of the twilight differently. SajdaTime cannot settle that and does not try to: it lets you choose the method your mosque follows, and adjust each prayer by hand if minutes still separate you. Separately, the app shows when a prayer becomes due; your mosque then sets its own later time for the congregation, so the board there and the times here will often differ, and neither is wrong.

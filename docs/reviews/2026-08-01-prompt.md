# Second-opinion prompt — SajdaTime prayer-time calculation defaults

*Paste everything below the line into Codex / Gemini / another model. It is self-contained.*

---

You are reviewing a design decision in a real, shipping Android app. I want a genuine
second opinion, including disagreement. Do not simply validate what is described — if the
reasoning is wrong, or a better option was missed, say so directly and explain why.

## The app

**SajdaTime** — a free, ad-free, privacy-first Muslim prayer times and Qibla app for Android
and Wear OS. It is built as an ongoing charity (*sadaqah jariyah*), not a product. There is no
business model, no ads, no accounts, no analytics, no server, and no revenue, and there never
will be. It is currently in Google Play closed testing.

Constraints that cannot be traded away — treat these as fixed, not as things to optimise:

- **No network at runtime, ever.** Prayer times are computed entirely on-device with the
  `adhan-java` library. The app must work on a phone with no signal. Calling an API such as
  Aladhan at runtime has been considered and permanently rejected. (Aladhan is used during
  development as an independent reference to check the maths — that is fine and encouraged.)
- **Location is coarse-only, foreground-only, never transmitted, never backed up.**
  `ACCESS_COARSE_LOCATION`. `ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION` is ruled out.
- **It must work for the masses**, including old devices, users who never change a setting,
  and users who cannot read English. When elegant and dependable conflict, dependable wins.
- **The app must never take a religious position on the user's behalf.** It is explicitly not
  a religious authority, and its disclaimer says so.
- The developer is not a scholar of fiqh. The app is built with AI assistance and the
  disclaimer states that too.

## The problem that was reported

A tester in Slough, UK (51.51°N) said the app's Isha time did not match his local mosques,
and that his mosques also disagreed with each other. Three mosques were named.

## What was measured (all figures verified, not estimated)

**1. The mosques barely disagree with each other.** Two of the three publish a *begins* time:
21:58 and 22:02 on the same date — four minutes apart. The third publishes only congregation
times in an unlabelled column, which is why it appeared to disagree by 47 minutes. Mosque
timetables mix "begins" (*awwal waqt*) and "jama'ah" (congregation) inconsistently, and
sometimes in the same table.

**2. Both mosques were identified exactly.** They use the **Moonsighting Committee Worldwide**
convention — *shafaq*-based, not a fixed solar depression angle. Verified across five dates
spanning June to July 2026 including the **21 June solstice**, where the printed Fajr (02:47),
Asr (18:43) and Isha (22:45) match the calculated values to the exact minute. They do **not**
switch convention in midsummer, which was the main risk.

**3. The app's default is Muslim World League (Fajr 18°, Isha 17°)**, resolved automatically
from the user's declared school. For Slough on 1 August it gives Isha 23:17 against the
mosques' ~22:00 — **78 minutes late**. At the June solstice, 23:30 against 22:45.

**4. But MWL is a good default for most of the world.** Tested against the regionally dominant
method in fifteen major centres, on a January and a June date. Isha difference, MWL minus the
local method:

| City | Local method | Jan | Jun |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jakarta | KEMENAG | +4 | +4 |
| Karachi | Karachi | +4 | +6 |
| Dhaka | Karachi | +5 | +5 |
| Cairo | Egyptian | +3 | +3 |
| Istanbul | Diyanet | 0 | 0 |
| Kano | MWL | 0 | 0 |
| Delhi | Karachi | +5 | +6 |
| Riyadh | Umm al-Qura | +15 | +7 |
| Kuala Lumpur | JAKIM | +4 | +4 |
| Casablanca | Morocco | 0 | 0 |
| Tashkent | MWL | 0 | 0 |
| Chicago | ISNA | −11 | −18 |
| London | Moonsighting | −20 | −47 |
| Berlin | Moonsighting | −24 | −43 |

**5. The boundary is latitude, not region.** Isha, MWL minus Moonsighting, at longitude 0:

| Latitude | 21 Dec | 21 Mar | 21 Jun |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20° | −10 | −7 | +2 |
| 30° | −8 | 0 | +12 |
| 40° | 0 | +9 | +35 |
| 50° | +15 | +26 | +49 |
| 55° | +30 | +40 | +31 |
| 60° | +55 | +59 | +7 |

Below ~40° the conventions agree within about ten minutes. From ~45° the gap opens quickly.

**6. Location precision is irrelevant to this.** A 3 km error changes no prayer time at all;
being wrong by 60 km costs two to three minutes. One kilometre is worth about five seconds of
solar time at this latitude.

**7. The high-latitude fallback rule is not the cause.** The engine uses
`HighLatitudeRule.TWILIGHT_ANGLE` on every method. On 1 August it changes nothing (18° is
still reachable at 51.5°N). It only engages roughly mid-May to late July. The library default,
`MIDDLE_OF_THE_NIGHT`, is unusable here — it collapses Fajr and Isha onto the same instant at
the solstice. `SEVENTH_OF_THE_NIGHT` lands closer to UK practice in midsummer than
`TWILIGHT_ANGLE` does.

**8. The app already ships the fix.** `MOON_SIGHTING` is in the method list and, when
selected, reproduces one of the mosques to within one minute on all six prayers — matching
Dhuhr and Maghrib *exactly*, because `adhan-java`'s MoonsightingCommittee carries method
adjustments of +5 min on Dhuhr and +3 on Maghrib. The problem was never that the option did
not exist; it was that first-run asks for sect and madhab and never mentions calculation
method, so a user who never opens Settings cannot reach it.

## What was decided, and what was rejected

**Decided:** two text changes only. No new screens, no latitude logic, no auto-switching.

1. Two paragraphs added to the first-run disclaimer: that mosques disagree about when a prayer
   begins, that Isha differs most and by over an hour in northern countries, that the app will
   not settle that question, and that the method is changeable in Settings. Plus that the app
   shows when a prayer *becomes due* while the mosque board shows the *congregation*, so the
   board will usually be later and neither is wrong.
2. An explanatory line at the top of the calculation-method picker, telling the user what the
   choice is for and to ask their mosque which method it follows.

**Rejected, with reasons:**

- *Auto-switching method by latitude.* The app would adopt a fiqh-adjacent position for the
  user, invisibly. The objection is to silence, not to intelligence.
- *Adding KEMENAG (Indonesia) and JAKIM (Malaysia)* — currently absent from the app. MWL is
  within 4–9 minutes of both, so this is motion rather than progress.
- *A per-prayer ± minutes adjustment feature.* Initially proposed to absorb what looked like
  mosques adding safety margins. Measurement showed those offsets are part of the method
  itself, so there is nothing left for it to absorb.
- *Precise GPS location.* Zero minutes of benefit, real privacy cost.

## What I want from you

Answer these directly. Where you disagree with the decision above, say so plainly.

1. **Is the "two text changes" decision right, or too passive?** Most users never read a
   disclaimer and never open Settings. Is there a way to reach them that still does not have
   the app choose a madhhab-adjacent position for them silently? Consider, and critique, a
   one-time prompt shown only above ~45° latitude.
2. **Is `HighLatitudeRule.TWILIGHT_ANGLE` the right choice for the ~10 weeks it engages**, or
   should it follow the selected method, or be user-selectable? Note that
   `SEVENTH_OF_THE_NIGHT` appears closer to observed UK mosque practice in midsummer. What are
   the fiqh and usability arguments each way?
3. **Is Moonsighting Committee actually the right recommendation for UK users generally**, or
   is that over-fitted to two mosques in one county? What do the major UK timetable
   authorities (Hizbul Ulama, Wifaqul Ulama, London Unified Prayer Timetable at 12°/12°, East
   London Mosque) actually use, and how much do they differ from each other?
4. **Is the sect → method mapping sound?** The app resolves Sunni → MWL and Shia → Jafari
   automatically. Is a *school* the right thing to derive a *calculation convention* from, or
   is that conflating two independent choices?
5. **Is there a materially better default than MWL for a worldwide audience** — for instance,
   deriving the convention from the device's timezone or region offline? What breaks when a
   user travels, and does that matter for prayer times?
6. **What is being missed entirely?** Look for the failure mode nobody in this description has
   named — for the Southern Hemisphere, the equator, extreme latitudes above 60°, Ramadan, DST
   transitions, or users who deliberately follow a mosque in a different country to the one
   they are standing in.

Be concrete. Where you assert a fact about a calculation convention or a mosque authority,
say how confident you are and on what basis, and flag anything you are inferring rather than
recalling. I would rather have three well-sourced points than ten confident guesses.
